# AI-assistance disclosure

AI tools, including ChatGPT, Claude and Codex, substantially assisted this project; their use was not limited to proofreading. Their roles included:

- generating and revising analysis code;
- debugging and implementing computational workflows;
- orchestrating and running automated analyses, audits and consistency checks;
- generating or revising code used to prepare figures and result summaries;
- checking references and numerical statements; and
- drafting or substantially revising manuscript and repository text.

The student selected the research question and study design, made the scientific and governance decisions, reviewed and modified generated code, ran the analyses, processed some data manually, evaluated outputs, responded to mentor/teacher feedback, and checked reported values against stored artifacts and cited literature. The student remains responsible for the work and AI systems are not listed as authors.

AI output was not treated as an authoritative scientific source. Numerical claims were tied to frozen project artifacts, and literature claims were checked against source records where possible. The remaining provenance, licensing and external-validation limitations are disclosed rather than filled by inference.

For ISEF compliance, this repository disclosure is necessary but not sufficient. Current ISEF rules permit AI as an acknowledged project resource but require the student's submitted research plan, abstract and poster to be in the student's own words and prohibit AI-generated citations. The student must independently prepare or rewrite those submission materials, verify every citation against its source, disclose AI use on the applicable Student Support Disclosure form, and confirm any stricter school or AUSSEF requirements with the relevant fair officials. This repository does not certify that those separate submission steps have been completed.
